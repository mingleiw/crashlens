Issue #1 of a series where I read every hot post on a topic, check what holds up, and keep only what's worth using. This one: Grok Bot for X growth.

The honest starting point

I read every hot post about using Grok Bot to grow an X account. The tutorials, the bot lists, the growth claims, the breakup letters.

Most of the claims don't check out. A few of the ideas are genuinely good. This is the sorting: what's real, what's noise, and the setup I'd actually use.

One thing up front: not one post in this topic proves a bot grew anyone's account. The closest thing to evidence is a single before/after chart showing +271 followers in 17 days.

Keep that in mind for everything below. Sources for every claim are at the end.

What Grok Bot is actually good at

The most common mistake is treating Grok Bot as a writer. The "let it write your tweets" posts are the ones with the least to show for it.

SIAxiom put it best: people batch-generate dozens of tweets a day and their followers don't move, because the problem was never how many tweets they wrote.

What the bot is good at is four separate jobs:

  1. Finding what's working: which posts took off in the last 24 hours, and why.
  2. Breaking down why it worked: hook, structure, timing, replies.
  3. Screening where to show up: which threads are worth your reply.
  4. Tracking your own numbers: which posts brought follows, which formats and time slots work.

The key move, and the one the best posts agree on: split these into separate bots.

One bot doing all four gets sloppy. Morlex learned this the hard way and rebuilt the whole setup around it. Finding, judging, and measuring are different jobs, so each one gets its own bot.

The setup I'd actually use

This is Morlex's build, with SIAxiom's workflow wrapped around it. It isn't the hottest setup in the topic. It's the most credible one: the most specific thinking and the most honest caveats.

Three bots, each with a "not your job" list:

  • Viral Scout: finds posts from the last 24 hours with 80K+ views, from individuals only. Brands, media, and aggregators are filtered out. Its job is finding, not judging.
  • Post Analyzer: takes what the scout found and breaks it down by hook, structure, timing, and replies. Then it drafts 3 post ideas in your style. Its job is judging, not finding.
  • X Analytics: reads your own exports over 28 days. Best time slots, best formats, which posts actually brought follows. Its job is your numbers, not anyone else's.

The workflow around them (from SIAxiom):

Find trends → research competitors → plan content → create → engage → review your data → repeat.

Two rules that matter more than the bots themselves:

1. Draft-only, always. No bot posts or replies under your name.

fave.xd built a 49-bot team with this as the founding rule: "not one of them can post or automate replies, that's on purpose. i approve, i post. no ai slop going out under my name."

2. Check the scout's work by hand.

Morlex's own warning: "the weak spot: scout can make up a post or a number. i still open 2 or 3 links from every batch myself."

Skip this and you're building on invented data.

The whole setup lives in one folder (Morlex calls it "growth on x"). Every bot has its own job and its own list of things that are not its job. That separation is the entire idea.

The 10-bot list, filtered

GBE posted the most directly usable list in the topic: 10 named Grok Bots, linked, all draft-only. They're all X-related, but they're not equally useful. My verdict on each:

Install these:

  • Growth Desk: drafts daily replies, text posts, and lead research. Draft-only, "never posts behind your back."
  • X Algo: tracks algorithm changes, so you know what changed and when.
  • Daily X Brief: a daily digest. A good morning read.

Situational:

  • X Community: worth it if you run a community. Skip it if you don't.
  • IdeaWatch: watches for post ideas. Overlaps with Viral Scout, so pick one.
  • X Feed Cleaner: indirect, but a cleaner feed means better inputs for everything else.
  • X-Man: a general growth assistant. Vague on specifics, right category.

Use once, then uninstall:

  • xDoctorBot: diagnoses your account. A checkup, not a growth tool.
  • X Score: scores your account. Same category as xDoctorBot.
  • Unfollower Bot: tracks unfollows. Real signal, low priority.

The catch: GBE's other list, "10 bots you need to make internet money," looks the same but only 2 of its 10 are about X growth. The rest are cold outreach, prospecting, SaaS spend tracking, SEO, pitch boards, CRM, and invoicing. Read a list before you install from it.

If you only want one bot instead of a system, take Melvin's pick: X Post Tracker.

It sends a daily digest of how your posts did, alerts you when one takes off, and watches viral posts with views per hour and milestones. Its card says: "Read-only: it never likes, posts or replies."

What nobody can prove yet

This is the part the hype posts skip. Every growth claim in the topic, and what it's actually worth:

Jacob Edmunds: "blow up your X account."
82,244 views and 1,202 bookmarks, the hottest post in the topic. But the post names zero bots (all six are inside a 23-minute video), and no follower or view gain is stated anywhere in it. The claim lives in the video, unverified.

Avid: "the closest thing to superintelligence… start a one-person company, grow your audience, pay your bills."
26,206 views. No bots named, no numbers, no screenshots. Pure assertion.

Marshall: "first day setting up Grok Bot got me over 350,000 views."
No analytics screenshot, no link to the post that got the views. The same thread says Marshall still needs about 200K more verified views to monetize. Worth asking how both are true.

GBE: +271 followers in 17 days.
The only before/after chart in the topic: 9,829 → 10,100. The Typefully chart shows +75 in the 20 days before and +271 in the 17 days after. Real, modest, and the chart alone doesn't prove the bots caused it. Still the best evidence anyone has offered.

Marcio Lima: the experiment to watch.
Subscribed to SuperGrok to see how far Grok Bot can grow the account. Starting point: 73,355 followers and 4.5M views a week. The experiment ends December 31. Grok itself replied predicting 100K+. No result yet. If anyone produces a receipt, it'll be Marcio.

The pattern: the most-saved posts have the least evidence. Jacob's post (2.26 bookmarks per like) and GBE's bot list (2.00) have the highest save ratios in the topic. Both are lists and promises, not proof.

The failure mode nobody talks about

The hottest post in this topic isn't a tutorial. It's a breakup letter.

Brad Groux, 327,536 views: "Elon, I'm a fan... but you just lost me on Grok Bot."

Brad went to bed Saturday at 52% of weekly Grok Bot usage and woke up Sunday at 100%, without touching it. The scheduled Sunday run of Brad's Chief Marketing Officer bot failed. Brad then withdrew the company's xAI partner application and scrapped dozens of planned Grok Bot videos.

To be fair, Brad flags the uncertainty too. There was a billing dashboard bug on November 1 that may have shown inflated usage, and the usage came back Monday morning. Whether it was a cut, a bug, or both is still unresolved.

But the questions it raises are the ones to answer before you build anything:

  • How long does the usage last? Marshall posted at 2% usage, then replied that it was "already at 45% since this post." Melvin's tracker runs every 2 hours, and people asked how much of the plan that eats. No answer yet.
  • What happens when a scheduled run fails? Brad's did, on a Sunday morning, with no explanation.
  • Why draft-only matters: if the bot can't post, a failure costs you a draft, not your reputation. Every author who insists on read-only or draft-only is protecting against exactly this.

The rule

Bots can find opportunities. They can break down data. They can filter which replies are worth joining. They can track your numbers better than you will on your own.

What they can't do is build trust.

The moment a bot posts under your name, you've crossed the line. The accounts that last are the ones that never cross it.

The useful parts of this topic are real:

  • Split the jobs.
  • Give each bot a "not your job" list.
  • Check the scout's work by hand.
  • Keep everything draft-only.
  • Watch the usage meter.

The rest is noise.
